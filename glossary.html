Understand the terms

Cybersecurity can sometimes feel confusing, especially when unfamiliar technical terms are involved. This glossary explains common online security terms in simple language to help you understand digital threats and protect yourself online.

Common Cybersecurity Terms

Phishing

Phishing is a type of online scam where criminals pretend to be trusted organisations or people through fake emails, messages or websites. Their goal is to trick you into sharing sensitive information such as passwords or banking details.

Malware

Malware is software designed to harm a device, steal information or gain unauthorised access. Viruses, spyware and ransomware are examples of malware.

Ransomware

Ransomware is a type of malware that locks or encrypts your files and demands money to restore access. Paying does not guarantee that your files will be recovered.

Data Breach

A data breach occurs when private or sensitive information is accessed, exposed or stolen without permission. This may include passwords, personal details or financial information.

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Two-factor authentication adds an extra layer of security to your account. Instead of relying only on a password, it requires another form of verification, such as an authentication app.

Encryption

Encryption converts readable information into a coded format so that unauthorised people cannot easily understand it. A correct key is needed to decrypt and read the information.

OTP (One-Time Password)

An OTP is a temporary verification code used to confirm someone's identity. It is usually valid for a short time or a single use. Never share your OTP with someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

Social Engineering

Social engineering involves manipulating people into revealing confidential information or performing actions that compromise their security. Scammers often use fear, urgency or fake identities.

Identity Theft

Identity theft occurs when someone uses another person's personal information without permission, often to impersonate them, access accounts or commit fraud.

Digital Footprint

A digital footprint is the trail of information you leave behind when using the internet. It can include social media posts, comments, searches and information shared on websites.

Spoofing

Spoofing is when someone disguises their identity to appear as a trusted person, organisation, email address or website. It is commonly used in scams and phishing attacks.

Firewall

A firewall is a security system that monitors incoming and outgoing network traffic. It helps block unauthorised connections while allowing permitted communication.

Smishing

A type of phishing attack that uses SMS text messages to trick people into sharing information or clicking suspicious links.

Vishing

A scam in which someone uses a phone call or voice message to trick a person into revealing private information.

Spyware

Software that secretly collects information about a person's device or activities.

SIM Swapping

A type of fraud where someone fraudulently transfers a person's mobile number to another SIM card to receive their calls or messages.

Password Manager

A tool that securely stores and helps manage passwords for different accounts.

Account Recovery

The official process used to regain access to an account when you cannot sign in.

Backup

A separate copy of important files or information that can help restore data if the original is lost.

HTTPS

A secure communication protocol that encrypts information exchanged between a browser and a website. It does not automatically mean a website is trustworthy.
